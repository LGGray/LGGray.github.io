Research

I study sex differences in disease with a particular focus on X chromosome inactivation (XCI). My current research aims to understand the role of Xist in maintaining XCI during embryonic development. I am also comparing the stability of the inactive X chromosome during cardiac aging across several omics technologies.

During my PhD at the Garvan Institute in Sydney, Australia, I investigated how dysregulated XCI may contribute to the female bias in autoimmune diseases, such as systemic lupus erythematosus.

My work is computational and I am passionate about applying rigorous methods to answer exciting biological questions.

Please reach out if you would like to collaborate or discuss research.

Projects

  • Gene Synth — listen to a gene A browser synthesiser that plays a DNA sequence as music. Each base becomes a note in a minor pentatonic scale, shaped by knobs for key, register, pace, tone and space, while a genome-browser strip scrolls the sequence and its pitch trace under a playhead as it sounds. Loads human XIST (GRCh38) by default, or paste and upload your own FASTA. Built on the Web Audio API — no backend, nothing leaves the browser. Live demo
