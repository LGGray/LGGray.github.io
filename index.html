Research

I study how X-chromosome inactivation breaks down with age and disease, and what that means for the sex differences we see in cardiovascular biology. Most of my work is computational: allele-specific expression from single-nucleus RNA-seq, hierarchical models of allelic ratio variability, and the statistical machinery needed to tell real epigenetic drift apart from technical noise in sparse single-cell data.

My current project uses BL6×CAST F1 hybrid mice to trace XCI erosion across cardiac cell types through aging and cardiac stress. The picture emerging is less a uniform loss of silencing than a widening of cell-to-cell variability — individual cells drifting apart rather than the whole population shifting together.

Before Munich I worked in Sydney, where I came to the X chromosome from the other direction — transcriptional signatures of X-linked gene dosage in autoimmunity — after earlier work on circular RNA in the brain. I work mostly in R and Python on HPC, and I care about analysis someone else can actually re-run.
